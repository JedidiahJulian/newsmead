# Researcher session steps

Use with the approved consent, screening and debriefing documents. This is an
operator checklist for the current app, not a new eligibility policy, questionnaire
or analysis protocol. Research approval is confirmed by the owner elsewhere.

Comparison-session order: consent and background/near-vision recording -> this person's
calibration -> practice/reading check -> Start run -> Reading baseline -> assigned
articles and questions -> Stop run -> survey ->
interview -> debrief and data-use confirmation -> transfer.

## Initial formative round

The owner has prioritized checking app usability, aid presentation and collection
practicality before a formal reading-benefit comparison. The manuscript's revised
Section 4.1 and Appendix A **Formative Interview Guide** describe this round.
It does not require a separate control group or two comparison readings. The
comparison checklist below remains for that later procedure.

**Before using this variant:** align the original consent, screening and debriefing descriptions,
which currently specify two comparison readings, with natural reading plus a
labelled preview. Research approval is already confirmed elsewhere; this is a
known wording mismatch, not a request to re-establish approval. The installed
app's Session instructions dialog still describes the earlier comparison flow.
The formative debrief must distinguish automatic natural-reading changes from
deliberate preview displays before data-use permission is confirmed.

1. Complete the existing consent, screening, this person's calibration and practice
   steps. Respect the existing retry/exclusion rules. With a Reading run started,
   collect the separate shared baseline if collecting the observational MD streams.
   Record the actual result, including unavailable references.
2. Use a code/label identifying this formative round and app version. Ask the
   participant to find/open the selected article, recording any researcher-handled
   steps or assistance. Set the actual Reading aids switch ON before normal
   reading. Record task start/end, pauses, completion, technical problems and
   actual/reported aid exposure. Do not ask for a particular gaze pattern, a
   deliberate loss of place, concurrent thinking aloud, or reading until an aid
   appears. The two bundled articles remain setup samples, not final study texts.
3. Leave the article and Stop run. Ask **questions 1-5**, recording written notes about
   natural reading before any preview. This checkpoint does not use interview
   audio under the current post-session-only audio permission.
4. Reopen the article, choose **Article options -> Article aid demo**, and confirm
   its preview/camera-off status. Opening the reader can start the camera before
   the demo becomes active; recording OFF alone does not stop capture. Explain
   truthfully that this is a deliberate display preview, not a gaze response.
   Keep study recording stopped. Note which aids were actually previewed, their
   order/settings and which interactions were tried. Leave the article afterward.
5. Ask **questions 6-8** for each previewed aid, then **9-10**. Use optional audio only
   for this post-task interview when separately permitted. No questionnaire has
   been selected for this round by the interview-guide revision; if a named survey
   is adopted, administer it before this final interview, then follow the factual
   debriefing and data-use confirmation. Keep natural reading, preview feedback
   and later evaluation data separate and link coded notes to the actual packages.

### Interview preparation notes

Read these before the session; the interview sheet itself contains short, open-ended
questions and follow-ups. Let the conversation follow relevant experiences rather
than reading every prompt mechanically.

- Record app/version, article/task and recording IDs in the session record. Use
  the participant code; keep names, consent and the identity key separately.
- Ask the main questions consistently. Follow up when useful, one question at a
  time. Accept criticism, no difference, no relevant experience, skipped questions
  and requests to pause or stop. Do not suggest that an aid must have appeared.
- Keep natural-reading responses separate from preview feedback and overall
  preferences. Record actual presentation evidence, reported noticing, preview
  exposure and unknown exposure separately. Ask only about tasks/steps the person
  actually performed. A preview-only user cannot assess natural trigger accuracy.
- Keep questions about comfort, distraction and readability separate when probing.
  Ask for examples and allow the article, surroundings or technical problems to
  explain the experience; do not assume the interface caused it.
- Keep the withheld trigger mechanisms for the factual debriefing. Explain the
  preview as deliberate presentation, without implying gaze detection occurred.
- Use quotation marks only for words captured exactly; otherwise mark a paraphrase.
  Missing answers, an aid not noticed and no occasion to lose one's place are not
  zero ratings. These questions are qualitative prompts, not a standardized scale.
  Everyday-use answers describe intentions, not demonstrated long-term adoption.
- Preserve the audio, disclosure, camera, phase-separation and debrief safeguards
  in the formative sequence above and manuscript Section 4.1. The short sheet does
  not replace consent or the researcher procedure.

## Before the person arrives

- Have the consent form, screening record, background questions and debriefing
  record ready. Bring the WHOeyes screening phone and a measured 40 cm aid;
  record the app version and device. The screening phone may be a researcher's
  iPhone or Android phone, separate from the Android NewsMead phone.
- Have the named practice passage and the assigned comparison articles ready,
  with their comprehension questions and scoring key. Choose the condition/article
  order in advance, following the approved counterbalancing procedure.
  The account-free build provides **Saved -> Testing articles** independently of
  feed refreshes. Its two bundled articles are setup samples, not yet selected
  participant-testing materials. Replace them with the agreed exact texts before
  relying on this list for comparison tasks; no shared Firebase account is needed.
- Have the actual selected usability survey and interview questions ready. The
  thesis describes these activities but does not establish a finalized survey
  instrument by naming them. Do not invent a combined reading/MD score.
  The [HCI assessment recommendation](reading-outcome-measures-evidence-2026-11-08.md#hci-assessment-recommendation--2026-11-09-draft)
  now proposes an English UES-SF and interview; it remains a draft, so its extra
  per-article questionnaire is not an adopted step in this checklist.
- Use a private participant code such as P001. Keep names, signatures, screening
  answers and any code-to-person key separate from the numerical gaze packages.
- Use the installed study build. Ordinary article gaze/FPS diagnostics are hidden;
  numerical gaze collection and the existing aid rules remain active.

## 1. Consent and screening

Explain the approved form in accessible language. Before the signature, ask the
person to explain what they will do and their right to stop in their own words.
Clarify once if needed; follow the approved capacity rule. Give the signed copy.
Obtain separate permission before any interview audio; the current form does not
authorize reading/practice audio recording through that checkbox.

Apply the revised screening guide: age 45+, habitual phone-reading correction,
reported relevant diagnoses, background questions and a held-out 150-200 word
practice passage. Record WHOeyes near-vision percentages descriptively, with
both eyes open at the measured 40 cm distance. Do not infer a Snellen grade or
apply a percentage cutoff. Reading eligibility is the ability to see and read
the practice passage at the fixed study settings with usual correction; record
the participant's report and any difficulty. An incorrect practice-comprehension
answer alone is not a visual-screening failure. These are study participation
criteria, not a clinical diagnosis.
Keep the person's usual correction, reading posture and lighting consistent.

Keep the two checks separate:

- Current MGazeNet uses 13 targets and 6 checks, with engineering PASS/RETRY
  values in centimetres. Save this participant's PASS calibration; the existing
  limit is two full attempts. This gate does not establish visual acuity or
  verified word/line accuracy.
- Study recording OFF prevents study packages, not ordinary article camera
  startup. Complete consent and save this person's calibration before practice.
  For the practice/reading check, keep recording stopped and Reading aids OFF.
  Then leave practice before starting the Reading run and shared baseline.

## 2. Calibrate the current person

Home -> **Calibrate eye tracker**. Complete and save the person's calibration
using the current screen instructions. Follow the approved retry/exclusion rule.
The saved tracker profile is not bound to a participant code: changing P001 to
P002 does not replace it. Never reuse the previous person's calibration.

Participant wording: "Please look at each target and follow the on-screen
instructions. We are checking whether the camera can estimate where you look.
Tell me if you need a break."

## 3. Start a run and collect the reading baseline

1. Open **Study recording**. Enter the participant code and first assigned
   condition label, select **Reading**, then **Start run**.
2. Return Home -> **Reading baseline** -> **Start**. Read the reader-facing
   instruction; the researcher handles technical setup and status.
3. Ask the person to read normally and scroll as needed. Honour a request to stop.
   This is a separate setup passage; it is not an automatic 20-second warm-up in
   the comparison article.
4. Use the footer's covered time and valid-window count. After at least 60 seconds
   covered and 30 valid windows, use **Finish** and inspect the actual result.
   An incomplete or unavailable reference stays explicit; follow the approved
   retry decision.
5. R/D/F readiness concerns the behaviour reference. **Raw ref: fit pending** means
   the coordinates were recorded for fitting after transfer, not that a coordinate
   model passed. Keep the same calibration/font/orientation for subsequent readings.

Participant wording: "Please read this passage as you normally would and scroll
when needed. You may ask for a break or stop at any time."

## 4. Conduct each assigned article reading

1. Open the assigned article. The researcher opens **Reading aids** and sets its
   main enabled switch to the assigned ON/OFF condition before asking the person
   to begin. Check this for **every article**. The run's condition label does not
   operate this switch. Keep other settings consistent with the approved procedure.
2. Record when participant reading begins/finishes and any setup, pause or change
   in settings. The saved visit includes setup/excluded intervals; it does not by
   itself confirm article completion or measure words actually read.
3. Say: "Please read this article as you normally would. There will be a few
   questions afterwards. You do not need to make any particular feature appear.
   Tell me if you want to pause or stop."
4. Avoid asking them to trigger an aid or explaining the withheld trigger mechanism
   during reading. Use the approved limited-disclosure responses when asked.
5. At the end, leave the article. Ask its prepared comprehension questions and
   record answers outside the gaze package, linked by participant and task IDs.
   Give the planned break. Record technical failures and requests for help.

For the next condition: **Stop run**, change its label and **Start run** again with
the same participant. The separate frozen baseline can carry across readings in
the same process while calibration/settings remain compatible. Check the actual
aid switch for the next article. After a process restart or calibration change,
collect the appropriate new baseline; do not silently substitute an old source.

## 5. Close the session and handle data

1. Leave the article and **Stop run**. Administer the selected usability survey,
   then the interview, then the approved factual debriefing. Interview audio is
   recorded only if separately permitted.
2. Use the revised debriefing script after the survey and interview. Explain the
   gaze-pattern/time-based aids and the observational MD measurements accurately;
   MD does not drive the aids or establish reading difficulty by itself.
3. Ask whether permission to use the data is maintained after debriefing and record
   the answer. Follow the approved withdrawal/deletion procedure across stored and
   transferred copies. Do not pressure the person to retain their data.
4. In **Study recording**, refresh saved recordings and select this participant's
   article readings. **Transfer selected recordings** includes available linked
   shared baselines. Verify the received packages before relying on them; preserve
   the original files as required by the approved retention procedure.

The camera-free UI checks passed, but a fresh live coordinate-reference fit after
the viewport fix has not been verified. The owner chose to defer that engineering
rehearsal. Keep coordinate status truthful; retain available raw data and other
outcomes without presenting missing scores as zero or successful fits.
